About

I am an Associate Research Scientist in the Department of Biostatistics at the Yale School of Public Health, working with Dr. Bhramar Mukherjee. My research develops semiparametric and causal methods for large-scale electronic health records (EHR), where the times a patient is seen, and the moments a biomarker is recorded, are themselves informative about health.

I received my Ph.D. in Biostatistics from UTHealth Houston in 2025, advised by Dr. Ruitao Lin, with a thesis on Bayesian adaptive designs for early-phase oncology trials. My work spans two connected programs: modeling informative visit and observation processes in EHR, and adaptive dose-finding for modern cancer therapeutics.

Research focus

Informative presence & observation in EHR

Semiparametric joint models that correct the biases arising when clinical contact and measurement depend on a patient’s underlying condition.

Adaptive dose-finding design

Seamless phase I–II designs that use biological mediators and survival endpoints to optimize dose for targeted therapies and immunotherapies.

Causal inference from observational data

Estimands and estimators for treatment effects under irregular follow-up, including dynamic regimes in real-world cohorts.

Variable screening & treatment regimes

Model-free screening for high-dimensional survival data that stays valid without assuming an outcome model or a form for the regime.
